Five wins from five, and the title decider is this Saturday.
APU notice, 9 October 2026

Outdoor sportssuspended

Because of the haze, all APU-organised outdoor sports and activities are suspended from 9 October until further notice. That includes training sessions, matches and outdoor recreation. Classes go on as usual.

APU is following advice from the Ministry of Higher Education and will post updates. Questions go to studentaffairs@apu.edu.my.

What APU advises

  • Stay indoors where you can, and skip outdoor exercise or long spells out in the haze.
  • Wear a properly fitted mask when you have to travel outdoors.
  • Drink plenty of water and keep windows closed when the air quality is poor.
  • The swimming pool for on-campus residences is closed.
  • If you have asthma or a heart or breathing condition, take extra care and keep taking your prescribed medication.
  • Get medical help quickly if you have trouble breathing, chest pain, a cough that won't stop or symptoms that get worse.

Community or Just a Group Chat?

Debates & Opinions department, Issue 1

Before I got here, “community” meant something specific: dinners that nobody skipped, group chats people actually used, a floor that felt like a family. That's the pitch, anyway.

APU has a large student population, they've got the receipts to back this up. Country clubs for every nationality, a math clinic, this very newsletter, more ways to “connect” than anyone could realistically use. On paper, connection is everywhere. But having the spaces isn't the same as using them. So the question stands; do people actually connect or just co-exist in group chats?

After a second cup of bad coffee and a couple of questions thrown around the university, the verdict has yet to be decided on APU's sense of community. A community could mean a group bound by shared interest, mutual support, a sense of belonging, effortless. Clean and simple. This definition was held up to our peers and here's what they had to say:

My friend, Zuhayr, an on-campus resident and social butterfly, believes the sense of community here is strong. His proof isn't a stat or a campus program, he mentions a sweet moment where he took a leap of faith with his roommate and his friends at an event, and it all worked out. His social circle expanded ever since then, and now, you never see him sitting alone. Ask him, and he'll tell you it just happened, no effort required. Which if you think back to that “should”; effortless, reciprocal, no one needing to ask twice, that means Zuhayr's got the pitch. He's the brochure come to life.

Where Zuhayr got the pitch, Richard read the fine print. My closest friend in cybersecurity had an interesting view on community and he points out a crucial part of the social fabric; his version of community isn't about more people, it's about the right ones. He's not interested in padding the numbers to make a floor or a club photo well.

Richard put it plainly:

“A community should be caring and respectful. Understanding one another and upfront, no wasting time filling in slots of people for the sake of size or letting random people in for nothing. If you want a community then fill it with people who can bear each other. Not rushing and doing whatever for the sake of having another person there.”

“People who can bear each other” not ‘connect with’ or ‘vibe with’. It's a lower bar and a higher one at the same time. Which makes you wonder how many of this campus's “communities” are actually full of people who can stand each other, and how many are just full.

Quality over quota. Nobody puts that on a flyer.

Maybe Richard is echoing something older than he realizes. Community didn't used to mean “everyone welcome”, it meant the people you were stuck with, for better or worse, until you figured out how to bear each other. We just don't have the “stuck with” part anymore, only have the “bear each other” part, and it turns out that's the hard part.

While some theorize about who deserves a seat at the table, Mary Kate pulls up more chairs. An active contributor to APU's clubs and also lives on-campus. She's got the friend groups, plural - not one tight circle but several overlapping ones. She knows her neighbors, volunteers for her country's events and not because of her resumé line, but because the opportunity presented itself. Mary Kate's not thinking about community, she's just living in the middle of it.

So what's actually missing here? Community, or the willingness to notice it?

The next few people asked to stay anonymous. Funny thing is, they also had the least to say, which might not be a coincidence. A quiet but active member of a country club told me while shrugging, “It's okay, there's not much to it, but I prefer it this way.” Another mentioned their small circle of friends then stopped, like that sentence had used up everything they had to say. A third, one of the athletes of the school gave a flat ‘yes’ or ‘no’ like community was a survey, not a question.

Maybe that's its own kind of data. The people with strong communities had names, clubs, neighbors, volunteer shifts, details they were happy to hand over. The people without much to say didn't just lack a good answer. They lacked the material to build one. No club to mention, no floor that felt like family, nothing they were proud of enough to put their name on.

Which is, in its own quiet way, the most honest answer anyone gave me.

So, is the sense of community here strong or weak? Depends who you ask, and apparently, how much they're willing to say.

Maybe that's the real finding, not that community is strong or weak here, but that it was never one thing to begin with. It's a patchwork, and whether you felt wrapped in it or standing outside it depends entirely on which square you happened to land on.

Four people, four different campuses. Same zip code, different postcard. Turns out there's room at plenty of tables. Nobody said anything about a seat.

Where to get help at APU

Mental well-being, welfare and disability support, and the Virtual Help Center: where they are, when they open and how to reach them.

Campus News department, Issue 1

Mental Well-Being

Sometimes the responsibilities of university life may overwhelm students, which is why it is important to have a support system within the university to help overcome those feelings of distress. Recognizing the student's mental health and well-being, APU provides a mental health center. The psychology center is located in Block E, Level 6 with working hours Monday-Friday from 8:30AM to 6:00PM.

To book a consultation with one of the university's psychologists, search on APSpace “Mental Health Support”, this will take you to a form where you fill out details such as your name, TP number and availability. The form will ask you if you give your consent to your information being used for research purposes; you can decline or accept depending on your comfort. You will then be given the option if you want to have your session with a professional or with one of the university's psychology students.

During this session, the psychologist will ask questions about your case and will also allow you to express all your concerns. If you are dealing with problems of addiction, the psychologist will refer you to group counseling, and even if you feel as though you want more help, you can ask for follow-up sessions.

Welfare and Disability Support

In addition to mental health support, APU has a Welfare & Disability Support Office at Level 3, Spine, Student Services with working hours from Monday-Friday at 8:30AM to 7PM and on Saturday working hours are from 8:30AM to 1PM.

At APU we believe students with different capabilities should all be provided with the same high-quality education and high-quality university life. This aid ensures that students with disabilities are treated so that they do not experience a compromised university life. A healthy university life encourages a student's academic and social development which is what we value most at APU. Disability support can include assistance with appropriate accommodation, including accessible rooms/kitchens, and accessible lifts.

APU Virtual Help Center

Aside from the mental and physical well-being help that APU provides, it also provides help with issues that may be faced while using Moodle, APSpace and Teams without having to be physically present at university. The technical support system provides a ticketing system where students can track their support requests.

Additionally, APU's helpdesk deals with things such as Wi-Fi issues, APKey problems, application problems or any general university systems. Moreover, you can access the IT Helpdesk on APSpace by typing “Help Center” into the search bar which will take you to a website, click on IT Helpdesk.

The APU Football Club is back

From underdog bronze medalists to undefeated league leaders, APU FC is putting itself on the map.

Sports & Activities department, Issue 1

After a period of quiet, the club has been officially revived under a new committee, and they are already making serious waves in the local football scene. With a fresh leadership team and a squad hungry for victory, the boys are on course for a great season.

First blood: the Xceed Tournament

The new squad's first matchups came at the Xceed Tournament, organised by Xiamen University. In a true underdog story, the APU team entered the competition without coaching leadership. Relying entirely on raw talent, grit and individual brilliance, the boys battled their way to the third-place playoff.

Facing off against the tournament organisers' Team B on their own home turf, APU took the game into extra time before clutching a thrilling 3-1 victory to secure third place. For a first outing with no official guidance from the sidelines, it was a statement indeed.

Total dominance: All-Star League Division 4

Fast forward to today, and the team has levelled up. Now competing in the All-Star League Div 4 in the Klang Valley, APU FC sits unrivalled at the top of the table with all 15 points from 5 matches.

A huge part of this dominant run is thanks to the addition of two dedicated coaches, Coach Kano and Coach Dwe. Their tactical guidance has transformed the squad into an unstoppable force. Just look at this winning streak:

Looking ahead: the UMD Cup

Regardless of what happens this weekend, the club isn't slowing down. Right after the All-Star League wraps up, APU FC will be stepping up to the semi-pro level in the highly competitive UMD Cup.

It's going to be a trial by fire. Our very first match is scheduled for 23 October against Visionary, the reigning champions of All-Star League Division 2, who went invincible. It will be the toughest matchup yet, but if the last few months have proven anything, it's that you should never bet against this APU squad.

Let's bring the noise this Saturday. Go APU!
